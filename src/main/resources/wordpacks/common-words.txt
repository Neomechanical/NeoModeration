# Everyday words by language, used to tell everyday words from one banned word
# split up. A banned word is not read across a space when every piece of it is
# an everyday word of one language, and that language is English, the server's
# "locale" or the player's game language: "put a torch here" stays clean, and
# for a Spanish player so does "vino Mari con su novio". A word on the server's
# own list only takes English or the server's language as its excuse. Pieces
# from different languages are still read as one word ("bean er"), and so are
# pieces that are not words ("f uck", "ni gger", "k y s"). A banned word written
# with a space ("ass hole") is always read across spaces.
#
# "<language>: <word> <word> ...". A language may take several lines, and
# several languages may share one ("hr sr bs:"). ShippedListsTest names every
# banned word that everyday words of one language spell, so adding a word here
# that makes a new one fails the build until someone decides. Slurs and swear
# words are never listed here, nor the words in languages.txt. Laughter and
# emoticons ("jaja", "kkk", "xd") are left out of other languages, where they
# would make an English insult look written in them; English chat words such
# as "lol" and "gg" are listed, so a message with them reads as English.
